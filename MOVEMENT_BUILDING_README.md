## Color Palette

The visualizations use a warm, solidarity-focused color scheme:

- **Coral/Terracotta** (#d4765e, #c96852): Individual struggle, challenges
- **Warm Orange** (#e88d6f, #f0a384): Mutual support, early organizing
- **Purple** (#a37fb5, #9270a6): Collective action, coordination
- **Teal** (#5fa8aa, #4f9799): Broad solidarity, strategic alliances
- **Deep Teal** (#3d8b8d, #2a6b6d): Infrastructure, powerful movement
- **Accent Gray** (#718096): Arrows, supporting text

**Design Rationale:**
- Warm colors convey hope, solidarity, and human connection
- Progression from warm (individual) to cool (collective) shows movement building
- Accessible to colorblind viewers (tested for deuteranopia/protanopia)
- Professional enough for policy settings, warm enough for community contexts

## Framework Structure

### Level 1: Everyday Struggle & Exclusion
Documentation, policing, clinics, schools, informal economy

### Level 2: Survival & Mutual Support
- Paralegal accompaniment
- WhatsApp emergency networks
- Faith & community solidarity
- Women & youth leadership
- Safe info-sharing hubs

### Level 3: Quiet Organising & Local Leadership
- Refugee-led orgs emerging
- Local alliances (markets, schools)
- Street committees / ward advocacy
- Trusted community figures

### Level 4: Collective Action & Coordination
- Coalition responses to violence
- Joint statements, rapid calls
- Cross-organisational workshops
- Movement convenings (e.g., MOVE)

### Level 5: Broad-Based Solidarity & Strategy
- Health justice alliances
- Housing & informal settlement orgs
- Youth & feminist organising ties
- Legal networks & advocacy groups

### Level 6: Movement Infrastructure Pillars
- Shared narrative + anti-xenophobia frame
- Leadership development (women/youth/migrant-led)
- Coordinating structures (coalitions, convenings)
- Pooled flexible funding + solidarity funds
- Community defence + rapid response networks
- Policy + litigation strategy

### Level 7: Powerful, Inclusive Movement
**For Dignity, Belonging, & Equal Rights**

## Editing the SVGs

All SVG files are structured for easy editing in:
- **Figma** (import directly)
- **Adobe Illustrator**
- **Inkscape** (free, open-source)
- Any text editor (SVG is XML-based)

**File Structure:**
- Elements are organized in labeled groups (e.g., `<g id="level-1">`)
- Colors defined in `<defs>` section for easy palette changes
- Text elements are editable
- Icons are simple paths that can be modified

**To customize:**
1. Open in vector editor
2. Navigate to specific level group
3. Edit text, colors, or layouts as needed
4. Save as SVG

## Context & Purpose

These visualizations represent a theory of change for refugee and migrant rights organizing in South Africa. The framework shows how:

1. **Individual experiences of exclusion** (Level 1)
2. Lead to **survival strategies and mutual support** (Level 2)
3. Which enable **local organizing and leadership** (Level 3)
4. Growing into **coordinated collective action** (Level 4)
5. Expanding to **broad-based solidarity networks** (Level 5)
6. Supported by **movement infrastructure** (Level 6)
7. Ultimately building a **powerful, inclusive movement** for dignity, belonging, and equal rights (Level 7)

The visualizations are designed to be useful for:
- Organizers and activists
- Community leaders
- Funders and supporters
- Researchers and advocates
- Training and capacity building
- Strategy sessions and convenings
